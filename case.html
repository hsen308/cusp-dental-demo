visit 01 · 40 minutes

Finding out how bad it actually was

The visible break was the small part. The X-ray showed the fracture running under the gum and the nerve already exposed — which is why cold water had been making her jump for a week.

We went through it on the screen with her: what had to happen, what it would cost, and the one part that could wait a year. She left with it written down and did not book anything that day. That is fine.

exam, X-ray and written plan — $25
visit 02 · 9 days later · 75 minutes

The root canal she had been dreading

Anaesthetic goes in slowly, over about ninety seconds. Almost everything people remember as "the injection hurting" is it being pushed in fast. She raised her hand twice. We stopped twice.

Nerve out, canal cleaned and sealed, post placed, temporary crown on top so she could leave looking normal. She went back to work that afternoon.

root canal, post and temporary — $260
visit 03 · 5 weeks later · 50 minutes

Matching a tooth to the one next to it

This is the visit that decides whether the work is invisible or obvious forever. Shade taken in daylight by the window, not under the clinic lamp — clinic light makes everything read a shade too bright.

Two crowns rather than one, because a single new crown next to a natural tooth almost never matches for long. That was in the original quote, not added later.

two zirconia crowns, lab included — $495
visit 04 · 4 weeks later · 20 minutes

The check that most clinics skip

Bite checked with the crowns settled in, gum line looked at, one small adjustment on the edge that was catching when she spoke. Twenty minutes, no charge — it is part of the job, not an extra visit.

She is booked for a cleaning in six months. Not upsold at the chair, just put in the diary.

follow-up and adjustment — included
